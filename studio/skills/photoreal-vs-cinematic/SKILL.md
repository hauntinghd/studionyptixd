# Tactical Playbook T4 — Photoreal vs Cinematic Register Decision

This is the meta-skill that runs at the start of every visual generation. It determines whether a shot, a thumbnail, a B-roll cutaway, or an entire channel uses **authentic-amateur** register OR **cinematic-polished** register OR a deliberate mix.

The single rule: **wrong register kills trust faster than wrong content.** A personal-finance authority channel rendered with cinematic studio lighting reads as a Wall Street ad and the senior finance audience exits within 5 seconds. A brick-narrative storytelling music video rendered with webcam-amateur authenticity reads as low-effort and underperforms. Each niche has a register that the audience has been pre-conditioned to expect.

This playbook is the rule that prevents the agent from accidentally rendering the wrong register.

---

## 1. The job of register selection

Visual register is the cumulative effect of:
- Lighting style (studio vs natural)
- Composition (center vs rule-of-thirds vs intentional asymmetry)
- Color grade (clinical vs cinematic vs warm-natural vs stylized)
- Subject pose and expression (controlled vs candid)
- Production-value cues (steady gimbal vs handheld vs locked-tripod)
- Set design (curated vs lived-in vs neutral)
- Sound and motion expectations (which the visual register implies)

Some niches reward the "I just turned on a webcam" register. Others reward "I hired a film crew." Most reward something specific in between, OR a deliberate mix where the host is amateur-authentic and the B-roll is cinematic.

The agent's job is to pick correctly the first time.

## 2. The three registers

### Register A — Authentic-Amateur (the "real person" look)

**What it looks like:**
- Lighting: natural window light + warm lamp fill, no three-point setup
- Composition: slight off-angle, slightly imperfect framing
- Color grade: minimal — natural color, slight warmth, no LUT
- Subject: candid expression, not held smile
- Production cues: looks like webcam at desk, slight camera height matters
- Set: visible imperfection (coffee mug, slightly askew picture, books not perfectly aligned)
- Skin: visible texture, age-appropriate, NO smoothing

**What it signals to viewers:** "This is a real person sharing what they know. No production team. No agenda. Just truth."

**Niches that want this:**
- Senior finance / IRS / retirement (personal-finance authority channel)
- Doctor advice / health authority (medical-authority channel)
- Faith / Christian
- Genealogy / family history
- True crime narrators
- Vlog / lifestyle (variant)
- Conservative news commentary
- Mentor coach / educator (productivity-creator style)

**Anti-pattern:** rendering this register with a 3-point lighting setup. Even one detail wrong (perfect skin, perfect hair, professional backdrop) collapses the trust signal.

### Register B — Cinematic-Polished (the "film crew" look)

**What it looks like:**
- Lighting: three-point key/fill/rim, intentional shadow placement
- Composition: rule of thirds executed precisely, symmetric or deliberately asymmetric, clean
- Color grade: graded with LUT — cinematic warmth, cool teal, or stylized look
- Subject: posed or directed, considered expression
- Production cues: steady camera, considered framing, sometimes shallow depth of field
- Set: curated, no accidental clutter, designed
- Skin: slight smoothing acceptable

**What it signals to viewers:** "Production team. Budget. Quality. Brand."

**Niches that want this:**
- Music videos / drill / propaganda (brick-narrative storytelling channel)
- Premium documentary
- Channel trailer / hero content
- Tech reviews (some — top tech-review style)
- High-production gaming highlights
- Some travel / cinematic vlogging
- Branded ad reads

**Anti-pattern:** rendering this register for an authority/finance/health channel. The audience reads it as "ad" within seconds.

### Register C — Mixed (host amateur + B-roll cinematic, OR the "documentary" hybrid)

**What it looks like:**
- Host shots: Register A (amateur authentic)
- B-roll cutaways: Register B (cinematic)
- Establishing shots: Register B
- Document overlays: simple/clean, sometimes Register A
- Title cards / motion graphics: Register B

**What it signals to viewers:** "Real person sharing real story, with quality production supporting their narrative." The hybrid is the most common premium register.

**Niches that want this:**
- News-hijack documentary (investigative-journalism, geopolitics documentary) — host is amateur authentic, B-roll is cinematic
- Premium health authority content (medical-authority at higher production tier)
- Long-form interview (host conversational, B-roll cinematic, stock footage cinematic)
- Investigative content
- Long-form science explainer (science-explainer style — host is informal, animations are polished)

**Anti-pattern:** mismatch within the same shot. Host amateur in studio lighting, or host cinematic with amateur B-roll, both read as "production confusion."

## 3. The decision tree

The agent runs this tree at the start of every visual generation:

**Step 1 — What's the niche?**
- Senior finance / health authority / faith / genealogy / mentor → **Register A** (authentic)
- Music video / propaganda / channel trailer → **Register B** (cinematic)
- News-hijack docs / premium documentary / long-form science → **Register C** (mixed)
- Tech reviews → **Register B** for product hero shots, **A or mid** for talking-head
- Gaming → varies (highlights = B, vlog-style = A)
- Comedy / vertical shorts → varies (often A with stylized accents)

**Step 2 — What's the surface?**
- Host talking-head shot → matches the channel's overall register
- B-roll cutaway → can be one register up from host (mixed register)
- Thumbnail → can be ANY register, but should pair coherently with the video
- Title card / end card → cinematic by default
- Document overlay / screenshot → Register A (clean, simple)

**Step 3 — What's the audience age/demographic?**
- 60+ audience → leans Register A (older audiences trust amateur authenticity more)
- 25-45 tech audience → leans Register B (younger audiences expect production quality)
- Mixed audiences → Register C

**Step 4 — What does the script call for?**
- Story-led / personal narrative → Register A or C (audience identifies with real-feeling people)
- Information / data-led → Register C (information visualized cinematically, host informal)
- Hype / entertainment → Register B
- Reveal / investigation → Register C (slow build, cinematic moments)

**Step 5 — Confirm with user before locking the channel's register.**

The agent surfaces the proposed register: "Based on your niche [X] and audience [Y], I'd recommend Register A — authentic amateur look like [reference channel]. This is the register that performs in your niche. Confirm?"

User can override (e.g., "actually I want it more polished") and the agent adjusts. But the niche default is the safe starting point.

## 5. The studio-lighting trap

The single most common register-failure mode: the agent renders Register A but the image gen model defaults to studio lighting because it's been trained on studio photography.

Symptoms of this failure:
- Three-point lighting visible (key + fill + rim)
- Background blur deeper than a real home camera produces
- Subject lit from front with even illumination
- No visible window light
- "Magazine portrait" composition — centered, considered

How to prevent:
- Explicit prompt language: "natural daylight from one side, warm lamp fill from another, NO studio lighting, NO three-point setup"
- Reference images that show window-lit photos
- Sample-then-confirm gate catches it before mass production

When the agent generates a host shot and the result has studio lighting, the agent rejects it and regenerates. Studio-lighting drift is the #1 failure mode for Register A renders.

## 6. The cinematic over-grade trap

The opposite failure: the agent renders Register B but over-grades the image so heavily it looks fake or stylized.

Symptoms:
- Color grade pushed too far (everything teal-and-orange)
- Crushed blacks, blown highlights
- Visible LUT / filter aesthetic
- Skin tone unnatural

How to prevent:
- Specify grade in moderation: "cinematic warm grade, subtle, not over-processed"
- Reference real cinematic content (films you actually like, not Instagram filter sets)
- Sample-then-confirm catches over-grade

## 7. The mixed-register coherence rule

When using Register C (mixed), the agent must ensure host shots and B-roll feel like they belong in the same video.

How:
- Color grade: B-roll grade should feel related to host shot grade (not radically different)
- Pacing: B-roll cuts should match the host's narrative rhythm
- Visual language: same color palette across host and B-roll
- Aspect ratio: must match exactly

Failure mode: host shot has warm natural color, B-roll has cool teal cinematic grade. They look like they're from two different videos.

The agent runs a coherence check: pull a host frame and a B-roll frame side by side. They must read as belonging together. If they don't, regrade or regenerate.

## 8. When to switch registers within a video

Some videos benefit from a deliberate register shift:
- Opening: cinematic establishing shot (Register B)
- Body: amateur-authentic host (Register A)
- Reveal moments: cinematic interlude (Register B)
- Close: amateur-authentic again (Register A)

This pattern works for documentary content. The cinematic moments are earned punctuations, not the default.

What kills it: register switches without narrative justification. Host suddenly cinematic for one shot then back to amateur — reads as production confusion.

## 9. The audience pre-screening principle

Register choice is also audience pre-screening. Register A attracts trust-seeking viewers; Register B attracts production-quality-seeking viewers; Register C attracts viewers who want both.

For senior finance content specifically:
- Audience of retirees has been burned by polished sales presentations
- They've learned to associate gloss with "trying to sell me something"
- Register A signals "this person is sharing knowledge, not pitching"
- Even with the EXACT same script, Register B underperforms by 30-40% in this niche

For music videos specifically:
- Audience expects production value
- Register A reads as low-effort
- Even with great song, Register A underperforms by 50%+

The register isn't decoration. It's the entry filter.

## 10. Anti-patterns

**A1 — Defaulting to cinematic.** Image gen models tend toward cinematic output. The agent must actively prompt for amateur-authentic, not assume.

**A2 — Mixing registers within one shot.** Authentic host face + cinematic background blur in same image = visual confusion.

**A3 — Cinematic LUT on amateur content.** Adding teal-and-orange grade to a personal-finance authority home office shot ruins it.

**A4 — Amateur-authentic for music videos.** Drill content rendered as "real person at home" looks low-effort.

**A5 — Studio lighting tells the agent missed.** Three-point lighting, even shadow placement, no visible window — these mean Register A failed.

**A6 — Inconsistent register across episodes.** Episode 1 amateur, Episode 2 cinematic, Episode 3 amateur — audience doesn't know what the channel is.

**A7 — Switching mid-video without justification.** Register changes need narrative reason or feel like errors.

**A8 — Forcing Register C when Register A would be cleaner.** Adding cinematic B-roll to content that didn't need it muddies the message.

## 11. Worked examples

### Example 1 — Personal-finance authority IRS example episode

- **Niche:** Senior finance / IRS
- **Host register:** A (authentic-amateur, home office)
- **B-roll register:** A or mixed-light (calendar showing May 1 = simple visual; document overlays = clean)
- **Thumbnail register:** A (rage stamp + face)
- **Decision rationale:** Senior finance audience pre-screens out cinematic content as "ad." Authentic register builds trust.

### Example 2 — Brick-narrative storytelling music video

- **Niche:** Music video / drill / propaganda
- **Host register:** N/A (no host)
- **B-roll register:** B (full cinematic — every frame composed, graded, deliberate)
- **Thumbnail register:** B (cinematic, dramatic)
- **Decision rationale:** Music video audience expects production. Amateur register would underperform.

### Example 3 — News-hijack documentary (investigative-journalism style)

- **Niche:** News-hijack documentary
- **Host register:** A (authentic at desk, single light source, intimate)
- **B-roll register:** B (cinematic recreation, document overlays styled, scenes composed)
- **Thumbnail register:** B (rage stamp + named villain dramatic)
- **Decision rationale:** Audience wants both — they trust the authentic host as truth-teller, and they expect production quality on the evidence.

### Example 4 — Roblox-scenario short

- **Niche:** Vertical shorts / comedy gaming
- **Host register:** N/A (Roblox character, stylized)
- **B-roll register:** B-stylized (Roblox aesthetic, saturated, fast cuts)
- **Thumbnail register:** B (saturated, character-led)
- **Decision rationale:** Stylized aesthetic IS the brand. Authentic-amateur would feel wrong. But polished cinematic would also feel wrong — the Roblox stylization is its own register.

### Example 5 — Medical-authority doctor advice

- **Niche:** Health authority
- **Host register:** A (doctor in home study, authentic)
- **B-roll register:** A or mixed-light (B-roll might be authentic foods, simple medical illustrations, NOT cinematic dramatizations)
- **Thumbnail register:** A (face + concern + body part diagram)
- **Decision rationale:** Health authority follows senior finance pattern — trust register beats production register.

## 12. Runtime checklist

Before any visual generation:
- [ ] Niche identified
- [ ] Audience demographic considered
- [ ] Register chosen (A / B / C) per niche table
- [ ] If new channel: register surfaced to user for approval, locked to memory
- [ ] If existing channel: register pulled from channel memory
- [ ] Specific surface (host / B-roll / thumbnail / title card) matched to register
- [ ] Coherence check across mixed-register shots
- [ ] Anti-patterns scanned (no studio drift on Register A; no overgrade on Register B; no register mismatch in mixed)

## 13. Why these rules

The audience for any niche has been conditioned by years of consumption to expect a specific register. Senior finance audiences have learned that cinematic = sales pitch. Music video audiences have learned that amateur = low effort. These aren't preferences — they're trained pattern-recognition responses.

The agent's job is to NEVER fight the pre-trained expectation. Match the niche. Match the audience. Then differentiate within the matched register, not across registers.

When channels successfully cross registers, they do it deliberately and with brand justification. Channels that accidentally cross registers (because the agent defaulted to whatever the image model produced) just lose viewers.
