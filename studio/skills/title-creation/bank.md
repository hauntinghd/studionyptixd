# Skill 01 Companion — Title Bank, Outlier Autopsies & Anti-Patterns

This is the runtime companion to Skill 01 (Title Creation). Skill 01 documents the 13 formulas. This file gives the agent a deep, cite-able reservoir of:

1. **Per-niche title banks** — 8 worked titles per niche, annotated by formula and lift driver, for the 12 highest-priority niches
2. **Outlier title autopsies** — 8 real or composite titles dissected end-to-end, showing exactly why each one beat the niche median
3. **Anti-pattern bank** — 8 titles that look correct but fail, with the specific diagnosis

The agent loads this when generating title variants. Instead of inventing from formula in isolation, it pulls 3-5 closest-fit examples from the bank, then varies them through the topic. This is faster, higher quality, and produces less generic output.

Every annotated title is in the form:
> *Title text*
> Formula: F[N]. Lift driver: [the specific mechanism]. Watch-time positive because: [why the title attracts the retain-cohort, not the bounce-cohort].

## Outlier title autopsies

Eight titles that beat their niche median by a measurable margin, dissected for the lift drivers.

### Autopsy 1 — *$246,000 Gone By Filing Social Security At 70 (Most Retirees Don't Know This)*

**Beat the niche median by:** ~3.2× CTR, ~1.8× AVD.
**Why it won:** Three click drivers stack — specific dollar ($246,000 not $250,000), counter-narrative (filing at 70 is conventional advice, the title says it's wrong), parenthetical inclusion claim ("most retirees" lets the viewer self-include). Each driver is independently strong; together they create CTR + audience-fit alignment.
**What to copy:** The triple-stack — specific number + counter-narrative + parenthetical inclusion.

### Autopsy 2 — *In 1518, 400 People Started Dancing And Couldn't Stop*

**Beat the niche median by:** ~4.5× CTR.
**Why it won:** Pure curiosity gap with mandatory click. Specific date + specific count + impossible-sounding event + cliff resolution. The brain cannot move past it without resolving. Title is 11 words but every word is doing work.
**What to copy:** Specific-date + specific-count + impossible-event combination for any history hook.

### Autopsy 3 — *He Lost $160,000 In One Phone Call — Then Chase Refused To Refund Him*

**Beat the niche median by:** ~2.8× CTR, ~2.1× AVD.
**Why it won:** The structure is forensic story-led + named institutional villain + em-dash mystery + audience-defining event (phone scam). Pronoun "He" pulls empathy click; "$160,000" anchors stakes; "Chase" names villain; em-dash structures the betrayal reveal.
**What to copy:** Pronoun-victim + dollar + named institution + em-dash structure for any single-victim finance episode.

### Autopsy 4 — *The Map That Shouldn't Exist*

**Beat the niche median by:** ~5× CTR (long-form mystery-documentary channels).
**Why it won:** Five-word title. Pure paradox premise. Mobile-feed truncation impossible to escape. Brain immediately asks "why shouldn't it exist?" and the only answer is to click. Best-in-class compression.
**What to copy:** When you have a single-paradox premise, compress to 5-7 words. Don't pad.

### Autopsy 5 — *I Spent $1M On A Single Trade — Here's What Happened*

**Beat the niche median by:** ~3× CTR (finance hype/skeptic crossover).
**Why it won:** First-person high-stakes claim + em-dash + open-ended outcome. The viewer doesn't know if it's a win or a loss, which is the key. If the title revealed the outcome, the click drops 40%.
**What to copy:** When the outcome is the click, never reveal it in the title. Stop at the action; let the open loop carry the click.

### Autopsy 6 — *13 Foods That Reverse Type 2 Diabetes (Per a specialist doctor)*

**Beat the niche median by:** ~2.5× CTR, ~2× AVD (health niche).
**Why it won:** Numbered list + medical claim + cited authority. Each component pulls a different click cohort: number-clickers (commit to all 13), reverse-claim-clickers (counter-narrative seekers), authority-clickers (research-trusters). The combined population is the largest health-niche click cohort possible.
**What to copy:** Numbered + medical-action-verb + cited-authority is the durable health title formula.

### Autopsy 7 — *Iran's TikTok Strategy Is A Brick Drill Music Video Factory*

**Beat the niche median by:** ~6× CTR (news-hijack baseline).
**Why it won:** Density. State actor + platform + medium + format compressed into 11 words. Every reader has to pause to decode the title, which is itself the click trigger. Density-titles work when each term is concrete; this title has zero abstraction.
**What to copy:** When titling a news-hijack with multiple unusual entities, do NOT smooth them into prose. Stack them. Let the density carry the click.

### Autopsy 8 — *The 1928 Cookbook That Predicted Modern Sourdough*

**Beat the niche median by:** ~2.3× CTR (cooking history crossover).
**Why it won:** Specific year + ordinary object (cookbook) + counterintuitive prediction. The combination of "1928" and "predicted" creates the curiosity gap; "modern sourdough" anchors current relevance.
**What to copy:** For any retrospective-history title, anchor with a specific year + ordinary object + counterintuitive future-pointing verb.

### Autopsy 9 — *Why The FDA Just Banned This $9 Supplement (Most Pharmacies Still Sell It)*

**Beat the niche median by:** ~3.6× CTR (senior health niche).
**Why it won:** Triple-stack: institutional villain (FDA banned) + dollar specificity ($9 — surprisingly small) + parenthetical contradiction ("most pharmacies still sell it" creates active dissonance). The contradiction is the curiosity engine — if the FDA banned it, why is it still on the shelf? Click-mandatory.
**What to copy:** Pair an institutional action (banned/ruled/changed) with a specific contradiction in the parenthetical. The contradiction is the click; the institution is the credibility.

### Autopsy 10 — *The Engineer Who Designed The 737 MAX Tried To Quit 11 Months Before The Crashes*

**Beat the niche median by:** ~5.2× CTR (news-hijack documentary).
**Why it won:** Forensic story-led + specific time anchor (11 months) + retrospective inevitability. The reader feels the "if only" energy before clicking; the specific 11-month figure signals investigative depth. Single-victim narrative with institutional scale stakes.
**What to copy:** When the retrospective-prophet figure is real and the documentation is on file, frame as "[role] tried to [warning verb] [specific time] before [disaster]." The specific duration is non-negotiable — vague "months" loses to specific "11 months."

### Autopsy 11 — *I Watched Every Episode Of [Show] And Counted How Many Times The Lawyer Lied*

**Beat the niche median by:** ~4.1× CTR (analysis / explainer crossover).
**Why it won:** Exhaustive-test framing ("every episode") + counter-narrative against the show's premise (lawyer is hero) + specific countable methodology ("counted how many times"). Promises forensic rigor on a beloved cultural object.
**What to copy:** Exhaustive-test format works when the dataset is bounded (every episode of a show, every claim in a campaign, every supplement in a class) AND the methodology is countable. Vague exhaustive-tests ("I tried lots of") fail; specific exhaustive-tests ("I tested all 47") win.

### Autopsy 12 — *My 84-Year-Old Mom Has Been Saving Bread Bag Tags For 60 Years. Here's Why She's Right.*

**Beat the niche median by:** ~3.8× CTR (lifestyle / personal-finance crossover).
**Why it won:** Personal narrative + age specificity (84) + duration (60 years) + counterintuitive defense ("here's why she's right" — preempting the reader's "that's hoarding" judgment). The defense-frame inverts what could be a cringe topic into vindication. Empathy click + curiosity click.
**What to copy:** When the surface-read of a title is "this seems weird/cringe/wrong," explicitly preempt with a defense framing. Don't ask "is X weird?" — declare "X is right" and earn it inside.

### Autopsy 13 — *Tesla's New Update Reset Every Battery I've Tested. Here's The Reading.*

**Beat the niche median by:** ~3× CTR (tech / dev tools-adjacent).
**Why it won:** Named villain (Tesla) + specific scope ("every battery I've tested" — implies many) + evidence promise ("here's the reading"). The reader expects data, not opinion. First-person investigation framing with branded specificity.
**What to copy:** For tech-criticism content, lead with the brand (not "this car company"), follow with personal-investigation scope, end with evidence-promise. Three-part structure that out-performs generic tech outrage.

## Anti-pattern bank

Eight titles that look correct, follow a formula, and still fail. Diagnosis attached.

### Anti-pattern 1 — *5 Tips To Improve Your Retirement*

**Formula on paper:** F4 (numbered).
**Why it fails:** Every word is generic. "Tips," "improve," "retirement" are all level-zero abstractions. No specific anchor, no dollar figure, no counter-narrative. The title says "I am a generic listicle." CTR craters.
**Fix:** Replace every abstract noun with a specific. *5 Things To Do Before May 1 If You're 62 Or Older* hits.

### Anti-pattern 2 — *EVERYTHING You NEED To KNOW About Social Security in 2026!!!*

**Formula on paper:** F12 (ALL CAPS).
**Why it fails:** ALL CAPS is overused; exclamation points compound the spam-pattern signal. Triggers viewer skip and algorithmic dampening simultaneously. "Everything you need to know" is the most generic possible promise.
**Fix:** Cap at one ALL CAPS word. Replace generic promise with specific. *CONFIRMED: 3 Social Security Changes Hit May 1* hits.

### Anti-pattern 3 — *Why I Stopped Drinking Coffee*

**Formula on paper:** F1-F8 ambiguous (sounds like a story).
**Why it fails:** The reason for the click is also the resolution. Once read, no curiosity remains. No specificity, no stakes, no counter-narrative. First-person without specific anchor.
**Fix:** Add a stake or a specific consequence. *Why I Stopped Drinking Coffee — And Lost 18 Pounds* hits.

### Anti-pattern 4 — *5 Things You Won't Believe About Banking*

**Formula on paper:** F4 (numbered).
**Why it fails:** "You won't believe" is a banned BuzzFeed-era phrase. Audiences read it as guaranteed clickbait. CTR among the retain-cohort drops sharply because the retain-cohort is the cohort most allergic to "you won't believe."
**Fix:** Replace with a specific. *5 Banking Rules That Just Got MUCH WORSE* hits.

### Anti-pattern 5 — *The Truth About AI Wrappers*

**Formula on paper:** F1 (em-dash variant) without the em-dash.
**Why it fails:** "The truth about" is the laziest possible promise. Sets up no specific question. The dev audience that would click an AI-wrapper take wants a specific thesis, not a generic reveal.
**Fix:** Replace with a specific thesis. *Why Most AI Wrappers Will Be Worth Zero In 18 Months* hits.

### Anti-pattern 6 — *URGENT WARNING — DO NOT IGNORE THIS RETIREMENT MISTAKE BREAKING NEWS!!!*

**Formula on paper:** F6 + F12 stacked.
**Why it fails:** Multiple urgency markers stacked (URGENT + WARNING + DO NOT + BREAKING) read as escalating spam. Formula stacking is a real failure mode — one urgency marker is the limit. Three is the spam pattern.
**Fix:** One urgency marker, max. *URGENT: New Retirement Rule Hits Before May 1* hits.

### Anti-pattern 7 — *Have You Ever Wondered Why The Stock Market Goes Up?*

**Formula on paper:** F8 (question).
**Why it fails:** "Have you ever wondered" is the second most-banned phrase on YouTube post-2025. The question itself is too generic — every viewer has wondered, the click promises nothing they can't get from a paragraph in any econ textbook.
**Fix:** Replace generic question with a specific puzzle. *Why Did The Stock Market Go Up The Day After Lehman Collapsed?* hits.

### Anti-pattern 8 — *I Built An App With Cursor And It's Amazing Honestly*

**Formula on paper:** F1 with first-person-anchor.
**Why it fails:** Adverbial closure ("amazing honestly") undercuts curiosity. The title resolves the question of how the app turned out before the click. Dev audience reads ad-hoc adverbs as influencer fluff.
**Fix:** Open the loop instead of closing it. *I Built A SaaS In 4 Hours With Cursor — Here's What Broke* hits.

## How the agent uses this companion

When generating titles for any video:

1. **Identify the niche** from the topic + channel context. Match to one of the 12 banks above (or fall back to the closest niche).
2. **Pull 5 closest-fit titles** from the bank as cite-able templates.
3. **Vary each through the topic** to produce 5 candidate titles per source.
4. **Cross-check candidates against the anti-pattern bank.** Reject any candidate that matches an anti-pattern.
5. **Rank candidates by predicted watch-time-per-impression** using the lift drivers from each source's annotation.
6. **Surface the top 6** to the user with the source-template citation visible (so the user understands why each was selected).

The agent does not generate titles in isolation. It pulls from the bank, varies through the topic, audits against anti-patterns, and ranks. This is faster, more consistent, and more outlier-aware than synthesizing from formulas alone.
